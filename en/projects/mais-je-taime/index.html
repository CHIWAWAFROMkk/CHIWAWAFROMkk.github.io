Mais je t'aime

He is heir to a gang; she has been undercover beside him for three years. On a rainy dock she shoots him dead — and, reaching him, finds his gun was empty and their ring clenched in his hand.

In one minute

The task
Tell a story with a twist in the length of one song; AI generates every picture, but hands, rings, scars and the timeline must stay consistent.
What I did
Script, storyboard, character and prop continuity; reviewing every frame and writing the revision notes; second-by-second video prompts; the edit, cut to the beat in Python.
Tools & AI
GPT image generation for first frames; Kling and Jimeng for image-to-video; Demucs for stems; Python, OpenCV and ffmpeg for the edit, desaturation and sound effects.
What you can check
The film and edit desk on this page (edit list, picture colour and waveform all taken from the film and its edit script), every clip, the revision log and the rejected takes.
Status

Rules first, then pictures

The music is “Mais je t’aime”. I split out the vocals, cut 60- and 30-second versions, then wrote the storyboard to the beats: vocals enter at 0:12, the shot at 0:44, the reversal as the music collapses at 0:52.

Characters

He: 42, blue eyes, curly brown hair; the break in his right eyebrow and the scar on his right cheekbone are one knife wound; the tip of his left little finger is missing (seen once, when his palm opens at the end); he smiles easily. She: 27, long straight black hair, cold eyes.

Prop continuity

Two identical plain silver rings. Hers is on her left ring finger: not yet in S08, on from S09. He takes his off before he dies and holds it in his left palm.

Review

Every first frame was checked zoomed in: each hand followed up its sleeve to whose hand it is, then which finger wears the ring, how many fingers, where the scars are, and the timeline. Of 25 final frames, 14 shots were revised; S17 took five versions.

Image to video

Each clip was generated at 5 seconds and trimmed to the script’s timecode. Prompts set out the action second by second — where every hand and prop goes, what background is hidden; the platforms have no negative-prompt box, so they describe only what should be seen. Jimeng followed character action far better than Kling and became the default. AI video runs slow, so each clip was retimed to its slot in the edit (speeds on the edit desk); Jimeng also warmed black-and-white frames, so the flashback was desaturated in post.

Revision log

Draft → final, and why each version was sent back

The commonest AI image errors are not ugliness but logic: hands that do not reach an arm, a ring on the wrong finger, a prop appearing too early, first and end frames facing different ways. Ten representative shots.

S01 The hand on the gun · The ring must be on the left ring finger

  • Draft 1

    The right hand holds the gun.

  • Final

    Mirrored into a left hand, which takes the ring to the left ring finger.

S03 end frame · First and end frames need the same camera position and direction

  • Draft 1

    Rotated about 30° from the first frame; Kling could only warp while turning to match.

  • Draft 2

    Direction right, but the deck plates and lamps did not line up, so the ground would drift.

  • Final

    Cropped from the centre of the first frame and enlarged 2×, pixel-consistent with it.

S07 Sharing a cigarette · Scars stay consistent across shots

  • Draft 1

    His right side shows, but without the broken eyebrow and cheekbone scar.

  • Final

    The same knife wound added.

S08 Tracing the scar · The prop timeline

  • Draft 1

    She only puts the ring on in S09, but here she already wears it.

  • Final

    Ring removed.

S14c The trigger · The action must really happen

  • Draft 1

    The index finger rests along the frame, off the trigger.

  • Draft 2

    On the trigger now, but her face shows, repeating S14a.

  • Final

    Hands and gun only: right index finger on the trigger, ringed left hand under the gun.

S15 Holding breath · The scar must be visible

  • Draft 1

    The scar on the right cheekbone is barely visible.

  • Draft 2

    Fixed once, still too faint.

  • Final

    The broken eyebrow and the cheekbone scar are clearly visible.

S17 She rushes to him · Every hand must trace back to its shoulder

  • Draft 1

    His face does not match the reference; the hand on her chest is as thick as a man’s.

  • Draft 2

    The hand is slimmer, but it comes out of his coat and cannot reach her arm.

  • Draft 3

    Hiding the hand on the chest made both of his arms disappear too.

  • Draft 4

    The composition is finally right, but the ring is on her right hand.

  • Final

    Left hand under his chin, wearing the ring; right hand on his chest.

S18 The empty magazine · Visibly empty, ring on the correct hand

  • Draft 1

    Ring on the middle finger; only the base of the magazine shows, so it cannot read as empty.

  • Draft 2

    Opening up and visibly empty — but in her point of view the ring is on the right hand.

  • Final

    Left hand holds the empty magazine, ring on its ring finger; right hand holds the gun.

S19 first frame · Serve the motion in the video

  • Draft 1

    Back of the hand up while the end frame is palm up: the video would have to turn the hand before opening it, and the fingers fall apart.

  • Draft 2

    Palm up now, but the curled fingers gained one too many.

  • Final

    Palm up, five fingers, facing the same way as the end frame.

S20 Vertical rise · Platform review

  • Draft 1

    Too much blood, too red: the video platforms might reject it.

  • Final

    The blood cut to a third and darkened.

Tools and process

  • First frames: GPT image generation; the script, storyboard and frame-by-frame review are mine.
  • Video: image-to-video in Kling and Jimeng, five seconds per clip.
  • Sound: Demucs to separate vocals from the backing, ffmpeg to cut the music; the rain and the gunshots are synthesised in Python.
  • Edit: Python, OpenCV and ffmpeg retime each clip to the edit list, desaturate, dissolve and dip to black, then normalise loudness.
  • Checks: Python and OpenCV measure rotation, first/end-frame alignment, flash frames and red pixels frame by frame, with fixes in post.
